# YouTube Channel Audit

Give a structured, prioritized audit — not a vague list of generic best practices.

## Inputs to gather

Ask for whatever is available and not already provided:
- Channel niche/goals
- Recent video titles/thumbnails (or links/descriptions of them)
- Upload frequency/history
- Any available analytics: views, CTR, average view duration/retention, subscriber trend
- Top-performing and worst-performing videos, if known

## Audit dimensions

Go through each dimension and give a specific finding (not just "looks fine"/"needs work" — say what and why):

1. **Packaging (titles/thumbnails)** — consistency of visual branding, whether titles create real curiosity, whether thumbnails are legible at small size, whether title+thumbnail combos duplicate information.
2. **SEO/discoverability** — keyword usage in titles/descriptions, whether content matches identifiable search intent.
3. **Retention signals** — from available data, note weak spots (low average view duration relative to length, early drop-off patterns) and likely causes.
4. **Content strategy** — coherence of content pillars, whether the channel has a clear "what do I get if I subscribe" promise, consistency of niche/audience targeting.
5. **Consistency/cadence** — upload frequency and regularity, and its likely impact on the algorithm and audience habit-forming.
6. **Community/engagement** — comment engagement patterns, pinned comments, community tab use, if visible.

## Output format

1. **Top 3 priority fixes** — the highest-leverage, most specific changes, each with the reasoning and expected impact.
2. **Full findings by dimension** — organized under the headers above.
3. **Quick wins vs. structural changes** — separate anything fixable this week from bigger strategic shifts.

Be direct about weaknesses; a soft audit that avoids naming real problems isn't useful.
